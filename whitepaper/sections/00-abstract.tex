\section*{Abstract}\label{abstract}

SlopNinja proposes an adversarial training subnet on Bittensor, pairing
mechanism \textit{A}'s AI detection with mechanism \textit{B}'s writing
transformation. \textit{B} is the main product objective: private models revise
authorized text toward the author's intended voice while preserving meaning,
readability, and tone. \textit{A} learns to detect AI involvement, including in
\textit{B}'s revisions; \textit{B} learns to remove unwanted formulaic prose and
evade \textit{A}'s detection. Public \textit{A} models supply training feedback
for \textit{B}, while authorized \textit{B} revisions provide challenging
examples for future \textit{A} training after evaluation retirement. This
competition is intended to improve both mechanisms; gains must hold on fresh
evaluation data. \textit{A} is also intended to supply author-fit feedback
against a private writer profile built from a user's own samples, once a
learned profile matcher qualifies. A lower detector score neither establishes
better writing nor changes a document's production history.

The subnet launches with \textit{A} alone as the initial evaluator and detection
service; \textit{B} activates after its editing, private-serving, and reward
requirements are qualified. \textit{A} is one text model with a shared encoder.
It reports estimated AI-written or rewritten text share and confidence in any
AI involvement as separate percentages, attributes human and model
contributors, and locates AI-written spans of at least 24 words, with
per-sentence share and confidence; shorter edits still count in both document
percentages. AI-involvement Brier score is the only reward quantity; share,
attribution, and spans are qualification gates, and once a cohort template
enforces it, every entry must also pass a span gate that compares its spans
with SlopNinja's first span package. On a sealed, pre-registered Test of
passages whose human documents are excerpts of 150 to 450 words, the
\textit{A} baseline's document model, the first reference, was noninferior to
Pangram 4.1, with balanced Brier 0.0518 against 0.0551 after both detectors'
outputs pass through maps fitted by the same method on a separate Calibration
split, but the pre-registered verdict failed because its three-seed recipe
missed one domain slice. SlopNinja's first span package leaves those document
outputs unchanged and adds a span head; retrospectively, on the consumed Test,
it reaches character F1 0.544 on spans of at least 24 words and puts a span on
2.5\% of human documents. \textit{A} reads raw text and uses no
context-free-grammar (CFG) features, which failed their first real-data test.
Private writer profiles use \textit{A}'s encoder plus stylometric features; no
profile tested so far is a reliable author-fit signal.

\textit{B}'s proposed private adapters run in miner-owned Cloudflare accounts:
miners pay their model bills but must not receive customer prompts or responses.
Cloudflare and SlopNinja remain trusted operators. \textit{B}'s editing
pipeline is planned to use a shared English grammar for typed word, phrase, and
clause edits and for revision guidance. Mechanism \textit{C} credits accepted
reusable contributions to that grammar once. It activates by its own decision,
which may come before \textit{B}'s so the grammar can grow first, and is not
required for the \textit{A} launch.

One lead validator, operated by SlopNinja, serves as the coordinator. The
protocol below states requirements, and Section~\ref{implementation-status}
records which parts run today: the coordinator is specified to run entirely
serverlessly on Cloudflare Workers but remains unfinished, and testnet cohorts
still run in a separate evaluation service. Miners commit artifact digests on
chain and upload their model files straight to the coordinator's private
storage. Each cohort, at least a day long, scores every entrant and the
current and previous reference models (when a previous one exists) on a fresh
hidden batch of paired cases, a fifth of them human-only. On mainnet its human
text comes from Common Crawl pages captured before ChatGPT's public release;
testnet uses a smaller public stock, and every testnet cohort so far has drawn
only documents already exposed. Entrant models are published, licensed
CC BY-SA 4.0, when scoring starts, and SlopNinja publishes its own reference
models on the same terms, without evaluation text. Exact scores never leave the
coordinator. It releases noisy quantized scores under a per-cohort privacy
budget and derives the native weight row from them by exact rules ported from
subnet 78's geometric score bands: detected copies of published models earn
nothing, and near-duplicate entries share one credit. Until an entrant first
clears the reference by a margin, miner rewards burn. Released scores are noisy
enough that an entrant merely equal to the reference clears that margin by
chance about a sixth to a third of the time on testnet
(Section~\ref{reward-v1}); at mainnet's planned size the current privacy
budget would make rewards mostly noise, so it, the batch size, or the budget
split must change before mainnet. The reference changes
when such an entrant also passes the promotion checks, or by a recorded
operator decision. The coordinator signs the row; every other validator
verifies it, commits it every tempo through native commit--reveal without
seeing held-out data, and keeps committing it through coordinator silence,
since the authorization it commits under never expires. Slow work and outages
preserve the cohort, no record depends on a chain runtime version, and no rule
locks the operator out: it can abandon a cohort, refuse to pay one, set the
references, or restart the record, each by a journaled decision with a signed
public record, the high-stakes ones under a separate offline key.

Testnet runs for several months before the mainnet subnet is registered. On
November 7, 2026 its admission opened to every registered hotkey, and a cohort
with an outside entrant has since run end to end there. Mainnet will open a
fresh record of cohorts with the reference testnet then holds. During testnet,
SlopNinja plans to earn revenue from a free public detection page served by
\textit{A}, with no advertising on the page that holds pasted text, and from
other products. Their payouts go into a publicly visible pot of native TAO in a
published two-of-three multisignature account with an independent third
signer, which lists every payout, conversion, purchase, burn, and claim. Once
the pot holds about 1.5 times the cost of a mainnet registration, SlopNinja
registers the mainnet subnet with it. After the subnet launches, the rest
buys its alpha in daily tranches bound to that registration: half is burned,
and half is distributed through a claim contract to testnet miners in
proportion to the testnet-era revenue earned by their winning models, which
must first have beaten the reference; any part of that half left undistributed
is burned. From the launch on, all revenue buys
and burns alpha; if SlopNinja abandons mainnet, the pot's TAO is burned. Customers
may also fund quoted paid detection jobs by card, TAO, or subnet alpha, whose
principal converts to alpha and is burned. Pricing, any emissions assigned to
paid use, and the mainnet split of emissions among owner, validators, and
miners are not selected.
